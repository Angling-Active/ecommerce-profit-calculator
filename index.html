How to read these numbers

Contribution and estimated profit answer different questions. Use the stack below to decide when ads are affordable — and when free shipping is quietly destroying margin.

Contribution vs estimated profit

Contribution profit is what remains after variable costs and advertising. It funds overheads and growth.

Estimated profit after allocated overheads subtracts a per-order slice of fixed costs. It is a management estimate, not statutory net profit.

Why VAT matters

If you are VAT-registered, a slice of what the customer pays is remitted to HMRC. Margins should be judged on net revenue, not the gross card charge.

Non-registered sellers keep the whole payment but cannot reclaim VAT on costs — enter costs as you incur them.

CAC, ROAS and break-even

  • Break-even CAC is the most you can spend on ads before estimated profit hits zero.
  • Break-even ROAS is the minimum return on that ad basis to break even.
  • Target figures aim for your chosen margin of net order revenue (default 10%).

Common margin traps

  • Discounting product while leaving postage and fees unchanged.
  • Free shipping when courier cost exceeds net postage revenue.
  • Judging ads on gross sales while costs and VAT sit off-screen.
  • Ignoring payment fees on the full customer payment.
